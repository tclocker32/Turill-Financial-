Concentrated Stock Planning

You do not have to choose between ignoring the risk and selling everything tomorrow.

A large low-basis stock position creates competing priorities: reduce concentration, manage taxes, preserve liquidity, respect insider restrictions and decide how much ownership you still want. The right answer may involve more than one strategy.

The Core Tradeoff

Risk of doing nothing

One company can dominate the outcome of the entire plan.

Cost of selling

Embedded gains can make immediate full liquidation expensive.

Planning opportunity

Sequencing multiple tools may improve the balance of tax, liquidity, control and diversification.

Potential Planning Paths

The public calculator shows the major tradeoffs. The private plan determines which tools actually fit together.

Immediate Sale & Reinvestment

Creates liquidity and diversification now, while realizing taxable gain on shares sold.

Staged Sales

Spreads diversification over time and may coordinate with tax years, price targets and cash-flow needs.

Rule 10b5-1 Review

For eligible insiders, company policy and securities-law requirements can materially affect the timing and structure of future sales.

Exchange / In-Kind Structures

Certain partnership or corporate structures can provide nonrecognition or deferral when strict requirements are met. Eligibility is transaction-specific.

Charitable Planning

When charitable intent already exists, appreciated shares may deserve evaluation before they are sold.

Retain / Hedge / Estate Coordination

The goal does not always have to be zero stock. The remaining position should have an intentional role.

Where the Online Analysis Stops

Some planning cannot be responsibly reduced to a public calculator.

That is not because the options are secret. The value often comes from how several strategies interact with your specific basis, income, tax jurisdiction, employer restrictions, charitable intent, liquidity needs, other holdings and long-term goals.

Your private analysis is designed to identify which strategies appear relevant, which should be ruled out and how the remaining tools may be sequenced into one coordinated exit framework.
